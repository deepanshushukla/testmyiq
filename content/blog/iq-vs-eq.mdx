IQ and EQ get compared constantly, usually as if one should "win." They
measure genuinely different things, and neither one is a replacement for
the other.

## What IQ measures

IQ-style testing targets cognitive ability: reasoning, memory, processing
speed, and problem-solving across domains like logic, spatial
visualization, and numerical reasoning — the kind of thing our own{" "}
<Link href="/logical-reasoning-test">logical reasoning</Link> and other
category pages cover. It's about how you process information, not how
you relate to people or handle your own emotional state.

## What EQ measures

"Emotional intelligence" was popularized as a concept by psychologist
Daniel Goleman following foundational research by Peter Salovey and John
Mayer, who defined it as the ability to perceive, understand, and manage
emotions — your own and other people's (Salovey & Mayer, 1990; Goleman,
1995). Unlike IQ, EQ doesn't have one universally agreed-upon
standardized test; different instruments measure somewhat different
facets of it, and the construct itself is still debated among
researchers in a way the "g factor" behind IQ largely isn't.

## Why the comparison is a false choice

Workplace and life outcomes depend on both, in different ways. A person
can reason through a complex problem brilliantly and still struggle to
deliver the solution to a frustrated stakeholder; another person can read
a room perfectly and still misjudge a purely logical problem. Treating
either one as "the real intelligence" and the other as secondary misses
that they predict different things.
