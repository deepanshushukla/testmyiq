A number sequence puzzle gives you a list of numbers with a hidden rule
and asks for the next one. The fastest path to a reliable answer isn't
guessing — it's checking a short list of standard patterns in order.

## 1. Check the simple differences first

<SequenceDiagram items={[2, 5, 8, 11, "?"]} />

Subtract each term from the next: 5−2=3, 8−5=3, 11−8=3. A constant
difference means it's a simple arithmetic sequence — add the same
difference again: 11 + 3 = **14**.

## 2. Check the ratios next

<SequenceDiagram items={[3, 6, 12, 24, "?"]} />

If the differences aren't constant, try dividing each term by the
previous one: 6÷3=2, 12÷6=2, 24÷12=2. A constant ratio means it's
geometric — multiply again: 24 × 2 = **48**.

## 3. Check the differences of differences

<SequenceDiagram items={[3, 6, 11, 18, 27, "?"]} />

If neither is constant, look at the differences between terms (3, 5, 7,
9) — if *those* form their own simple pattern (here, increasing by 2 each
time), the next difference is 11, giving 27 + 11 = **38**. This is the
same sample question featured on our{" "}
<Link href="/numerical-reasoning-test">numerical reasoning test page</Link>.

## 4. Check for two interleaved sequences

<SequenceDiagram items={[1, 10, 2, 20, 3, "?"]} />

Sometimes what looks like one sequence is really two, alternating:
positions 1, 3, 5… form 1, 2, 3… and positions 2, 4, 6… form 10, 20, 30….
The next term (position 6) continues the second sequence: **30**.

## The general method

Work through these checks in order — constant difference, constant
ratio, second-order difference, then interleaving — before considering
anything more exotic. Most sequence puzzles, including the ones in our
own question bank, use one of these four patterns.
